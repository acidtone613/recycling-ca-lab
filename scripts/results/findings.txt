The coupling is real — but it acts on settling and activity, not on extinction.

1. Extinction never happens, under any coupling, at any density (0% joint extinction across all 5,600 runs of the 2D sweep, baselines included). On a 64×64 torus, Life-like rules from random initial states always leave still-life and oscillator mosaics, so the all-0 absorbing state is practically unreachable. The hypothesized "lossy → sharp extinction threshold" is therefore not observable in this regime: for the extinction observable, the CA rule dominates the coupling.

2. What recycling decisively changes is whether the system settles at all. Uncoupled layers detected limit cycles in 39–69% of runs (median steady-state activity 10–20 ×10⁻³ per cell; standing union density ≈ 0.06). Every coupled configuration suppressed cycle detection to ~0% and stayed active for the full 1,200-step cap — with 3–12× the baseline activity and 2–4× the standing density (union ρ up to 0.25). The mechanism is visible in the event accounting: a settled Life mosaic still produces ~81 deaths/step in Layer 1's oscillators, and those deaths perpetually re-seed Layer 2, so the joint state never repeats.

3. Topology orders the effect exactly as predicted — non-local recycling maximises the shift. At density 0.3 (Life/Life): same-coordinate + drop delivers 56 recycled births/step (losing 25 to collisions, 31%) for union ρ 0.15 and activity 60×10⁻³; random-empty delivers ~81/step for ρ 0.19 and activity 90×10⁻³. Overwrite (guaranteed delivery) converts the same-coordinate losses into redirects, lifting its activity above drop (70 vs 60 ×10⁻³). The Life → HighLife pair amplifies everything (random-empty: activity 120×10⁻³, ρ 0.25).

4. Where extinction IS the attractor, recycling modulates survival time in the same topology order. Rule 90 on a 128-cell ring is nilpotent — every state collapses to 0 (baseline median extinction step 64). Same-coordinate recycling stretches the median to 118 steps and random-empty to 126 (≈ the rule's nilpotency index), while nearest-empty is re-absorbed locally (65). Non-local recycling nearly doubles the lifetime of a system doomed to die.

5. The cleanest attractor-class change: W110 → W90. Layer 2 (W90) alone goes extinct in 100% of runs. Coupled to a W110 source, it never dies (0%), held at ~0.60 live density (median ≈77 of 128 cells; union density ≈0.86) entirely by Layer 1's perpetual death stream — joint activity ≈2.2× the uncoupled baseline (≈470 vs ≈210 ×10⁻³).

6. The structural theorem is visible in the data: Layer 1's death rate (~81/step at density 0.3) is identical across every topology and collision policy — unidirectional recycling cannot feed back into its source. All coupling effects live in Layer 2 and the joint observables, exactly as the L1-invariance theorem predicts.

Honest caveats: "active @ cap" for stochastic recycling (random-empty topology, overwrite collision) means sustained non-equilibrium activity — those processes are Markov chains, not deterministic CAs, so strict limit cycles are not well-defined for them (cycle detection is RNG-tagged and, correctly, never fires). The extinction result is regime-specific: still-life-free rules (e.g. Seeds), smaller grids, or fixed boundaries could restore an observable extinction threshold — the natural next experiment, and the workbench is built to run it.
